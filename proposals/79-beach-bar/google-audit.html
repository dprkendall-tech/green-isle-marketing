Priority 1 · Remove friction

  1. Confirm ownership and manager access. The owner should retain primary ownership. A marketing manager can use their own authorised account; there is no reason to share a password.
  2. Add the finished website. Use a clean business or demo domain for the proposal, then the owner-approved production URL in Google. Keep a proposal separate from the final public customer site.
  3. Publish the complete current menu. Add menu sections, actual dishes, descriptions and prices, plus readable menu photographs and a full-menu URL. Use English and Thai where practical. A gallery of food highlights is not a full menu.
  4. Make booking real before using a booking action. The present demo prepares an email/Facebook request. Google requires action links to let guests complete the relevant action; messaging links are not accepted as business action links. Connect an eligible reservation provider or a proper reservation page with the owner’s operating rules. Do not advertise instant confirmation while staff still need to approve requests.
  5. Standardise core facts. Confirm and align name, address, telephone and hours across Google, Facebook, Tripadvisor and the website. Check the previous location/name for outdated entries; do not delete or merge listings without establishing the facts.

Priority 2 · Make the listing persuasive

Better lead photography

Upload an owner-approved sea-facing dining photograph with an uncluttered table and visible beach. Add a small, carefully chosen set: a sunset view, daytime view, evening lighting, four signature dishes, the team, entrance and seating options. Use original files where possible and avoid heavy filters.

Select a cover photo, but do not promise it will always appear first: Google can choose another image. Do not try to remove legitimate customer photos merely because they are less flattering.

Accurate categories and attributes

Check whether “Restaurant” remains the best primary category. Consider “Thai restaurant” if that accurately describes the main business. Consider “Bar” and “Seafood restaurant” as additional categories only where available and representative.

Confirm outdoor seating, dine-in, takeaway, reservations, payment methods, Wi-Fi, toilets, parking, accessibility and highchairs before selecting attributes. Do not infer accessibility from the presence of beach seating.

Keep the real business name. Do not append “best sunset restaurant Krabi” or other search phrases to the listing name.

Suggested business description

79 Beach Bar & Restaurant is a beachfront restaurant on Koh Kwang Beach in Klong Muang, Krabi. Enjoy Thai food, seafood and relaxed dining with sea views, from leisurely lunches to evenings beside the water. Whether you’re visiting on holiday or live nearby, come and make yourself comfortable. Contact the team for the current menu and table availability.

Draft for owner approval, within Google’s 750-character limit. It deliberately excludes unverified prices, event schedules and promotional claims.

Priority 3 · Protect the reputation

The visible positive reviews repeatedly support the food, setting and welcome. A secondary source reproduces a Google complaint about lengthy food waits and an owner reply explaining peak-period pressure. Tripadvisor also contains criticism about waits and dishes not matching expectations. These are individual reports, not a finding that all service is poor.

  • Review the newest and lowest-rated feedback in the owner dashboard; the public session did not permit a complete chronological review.
  • Respond promptly, calmly and specifically. Acknowledge the experience, explain only what is known and state a remedy only when it has actually happened.
  • Check kitchen capacity at sunset, especially with groups. Quote realistic waiting times before taking orders, and match portion photographs to what guests receive.
  • Offer every guest the same opportunity to review, using Google’s direct review link or QR code. Do not reward reviews or route only happy customers to public platforms.
  • Keep private feedback available as an additional contact option, not as a replacement for public reviewing when someone is unhappy.

Operational improvements and clearer expectations will support the marketing. More advertising alone will not solve slow service on a busy evening.

Priority 4 · Build local discovery

  • Local website signals: use an accurate page title such as “79 Beach Bar & Restaurant | Klong Muang, Krabi”; place the confirmed address, telephone, hours and full menu in readable text. Add valid Restaurant structured data using only confirmed facts.
  • Indexing: the current proposal is intentionally noindexed. Remove that restriction only from the approved production customer pages; keep internal proposal and audit pages out of search. Verify the production domain in Search Console and submit its sitemap.
  • Useful updates: publish real food, team and venue content regularly. Add events only after dates and delivery are confirmed. There is no guaranteed ranking bonus from an arbitrary number of posts.
  • Referral partners: give nearby hotels, villa and condo hosts a current menu, location and reservation link. Measure resulting visits; do not buy links or fabricate local references.
  • Measure a baseline: record available Business Profile metrics for searches, calls, website clicks, directions, menu activity and bookings. Compare changes over time, allowing for seasonal demand and weather.
  • Track attended bookings: add source tags to website links and ask diners how they found 79. Website traffic, directions and calls are useful signals, not proof of revenue.

Google’s local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Optimisation can improve the profile’s usefulness and visibility; it cannot guarantee first place for every searcher.
